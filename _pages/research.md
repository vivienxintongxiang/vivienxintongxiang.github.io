## Job Market Paper

### “Certified but Narrow: Personal Certification, Depositor Trust, and the Retreat from Delegated Monitoring”

**Abstract.** This study examines the effect of personal certification of bank compliance on the discretionary behavior of treated banks. In 2015, the New York Department of Financial Services proposed a regulation requiring a named senior officer of every state-chartered bank to certify the bank’s anti-money laundering program, with personal criminal liability for an incorrect certification. I first validate that, following the proposal, treated banks replace their own judgment with rules where rules exist and withdraw where none exist: the share of mortgages below the 620 credit score threshold and the share of SBA loans approved under delegated authority both decline, and small business lending contracts. I then show that this retreat from discretion is rewarded on the liability side: severe enforcement actions decline, and depositors reallocate their funds from local competitors to treated banks at unchanged deposit rates, a local reallocation effect visible in both insured and uninsured deposits. Both effects are more pronounced for banks with greater ex ante enforcement exposure. The additional funding, however, is retained as cash. Counties most exposed to treated banks experience a decline in small business credit that other lenders do not replace, slower establishment growth, and higher unemployment, a cost borne by borrowers whom only discretion can serve.
