## Job Market Paper

### “Certified but Narrow: Personal Certification, Depositor Information Acquisition, and the Retreat from Discretionary Lending”

**Presented at:** HKAAA (2026); 39th Australasian Finance and Banking Conference (2026); Sydney Banking and Financial Stability Conference (2026)

**Abstract.** This study examines how personal certification of bank compliance affects depositors' information acquisition and deposit flows. In 2015, the New York Department of Financial Services proposed a regulation requiring the chief compliance officer of every New York-chartered bank to personally certify its anti-money-laundering compliance, with potential criminal penalties for a false certification. I first validate that certified banks experience a decline in severe enforcement actions following the proposal. I then show that depositors reallocate funds from local uncertified competitors to certified banks at unchanged deposit rates, a local reallocation effect that is more pronounced for banks with greater pre-existing enforcement exposure. Consistent with public certification crowding out depositors' private information acquisition, deposits at certified banks become insensitive to money-laundering news: when such news appears in a county, uncertified branches lose deposits while certified branches in the same county do not. Additional analyses reveal the cost of producing the certified signal: certified banks retreat from discretionary lending and reduce small business credit in the counties they serve. Overall, the findings indicate that certification both attracts deposits and stabilizes bank funding by reducing depositors' need to monitor, with the unintended cost of credit to borrowers who depend on bank discretion.
